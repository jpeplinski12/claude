Yarin was patrolling Israel’s Highway 60 when a terrorist rammed him with a car at high speed. Broken bones. Brain damage. Internal bleeding. His leg nearly severed.

Magen David Adom paramedic Yair Schussheim arrived within moments. “When I first saw him, I did not think he would survive.”

But Yair’s training kicked in. In Israel, paramedics receive nearly 10 times the training of standard EMTs—preparing them for the worst emergencies imaginable. He loaded Yarin into a mobile ICU, opened two IV lines, and kept him alive minute by minute.

“I told the driver, ‘Floor the gas! Don’t stop!’”

Dr. Miklosh Bala, the trauma surgeon who treated Yarin, credits Yair’s quick decisions as the key to survival. Had he waited even minutes more, Yarin would have died.

Six months later, Yarin attended a ceremony honoring the paramedic who saved his life. “Yair is not just the paramedic who saved me. He is like my brother.”
